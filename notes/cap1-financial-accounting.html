Question 8
A mutual fund that had a net asset value of Rs. 16 at the beginning of a month, made income and capital gain distribution of Rs. 0.04 and Rs. 0.03 respectively per unit during the month, and then ended the month with a net asset value of Rs. 16.08. Calculate monthly and annual rate of return.
Ans: 0.9375% ; 11.25% p.a.
Return (%) = (NAV₁ – NAV₀) + D₁NAV₀ × 100 = (16.08 – 16) + 0.04 + 0.0316 × 100 = 0.9375% per month
Annual return = 0.9375 × 12 = 11.25% p.a.
✦ What makes this question special

This is your first return question — it introduces the core formula. D₁ includes both income distribution and capital gain distribution; both are cash received by the investor so both go in the numerator.

Annualization: if holding period is in months, multiply by (12 ÷ months). If in days, multiply by (365 ÷ days). When the period is short and exact days can be counted, the notes say the exam prefers the days method.

The following information is extracted from Steady Mutual Fund's Scheme:
  • Asset Value at the beginning of the month – Rs. 65.78
  • Annualized return – 15%
  • Distributions made in the nature of Income – Rs.0.50 and Rs.0.32 as Capital gain (per unit respectively).
You are required to: (1) Calculate the month end net asset value of the mutual fund scheme (limit your answers to two decimals). (2) Provide a brief comment on the month end NAV.

Question 10
The NAV of each unit of a closed-end fund at the beginning of the year was 18. By the end of the year its NAV equals 18.50. At the beginning of the year each unit was selling at a 2% premium to NAV and by the end of the year each unit is selling at 4% discount to NAV.

If the closed-end fund paid year end distribution of income of Rs. 2.50 on each unit, find the rate of return to the investor in the fund during the year.
Ans: 10.35% p.a.
Since this is a close-ended fund, P₀ and P₁ are market prices (not NAV)
P₀ = 18 + 2% of 18 = Rs. 18.36
P₁ = 18.50 – 4% of 18.50 = Rs. 17.76
Return = (17.76 – 18.36) + 2.5018.36 × 100 = 10.35% p.a.
✦ What makes this question special

Close-ended funds trade on the stock exchange, so the investor buys and sells at market price — not at NAV. P₀ and P₁ are market prices calculated from NAV ± premium/discount. Using NAV directly as P₀ and P₁ is the classic mistake here.

Notice the fund's NAV actually went up (18 → 18.50) but the investor's return is modest because the market sentiment worsened — it moved from a 2% premium to a 4% discount. The underlying portfolio improved but the investor was hurt by the sentiment shift.

Question 11
An investor purchased 300 units of a Mutual Fund at Rs.12.25 per unit on 31st December, 2009. As on 31st December, 2010 he has received Rs.1.25 as dividend and Rs.1.00 as capital gains distribution per unit.
Required: (i) The return on the investment if the NAV as on 31st December, 2010 is Rs.13.00. (ii) The return on the investment as on 31st December, 2010 if all dividends and capital gains distributions are reinvested into additional units of the fund at Rs.12.50 per unit.
Ans: (i) 24.49%   (ii) 25.22%
Part (i): Cash payout
Return = (13 – 12.25) + 1.25 + 1.0012.25 × 100 = 24.49% p.a.
Part (ii): Reinvestment plan
Total distribution = 300 × 2.25 = Rs. 675  →  Additional units = 675 ÷ 12.50 = 54 units
Total units = 300 + 54 = 354
Return = (354 × 13) – (300 × 12.25)300 × 12.25 × 100 = 25.22% p.a.
