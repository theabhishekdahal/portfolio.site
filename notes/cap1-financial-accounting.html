CAP I — Financial Accounting: Key Concepts

📅 1 March 2025 📂 Financial Accounting 🏷️ CAP I · Double Entry · Trial Balance

This note summarises the core concepts tested in the CAP I Financial Accounting paper set by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nepal (ICAN). The topics below cover approximately 60–70 % of the marks in a typical exam.

1. The Accounting Equation

All of accounting rests on one identity:

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity

Every transaction must leave this equation balanced. When cash is received from a customer, Assets ↑ (Cash) and Liabilities/Equity ↑ (Revenue → Retained Earnings).

2. Double-Entry Bookkeeping

Every transaction is recorded in at least two accounts: a debit in one account and an equal credit in another.

3. Journals, Ledgers & Trial Balance

  • Journal — chronological record of every transaction (date, accounts, amounts, narration).
  • Ledger — classifies all journal entries by account (T-accounts).
  • Trial Balance — list of all ledger balances; total debits must equal total credits.

Tip: Always verify the trial balance before preparing financial statements. A balanced trial balance does not guarantee the absence of errors (e.g., compensating errors).

4. Adjusting Entries (End-of-Period)

Adjusting entries ensure revenues are recognised when earned and expenses when incurred (accrual basis).

5. The Financial Statements

Four inter-linked statements are prepared at period-end:

  1. Income Statement (Profit & Loss) — Revenues − Expenses = Net Profit / Loss
  2. Statement of Changes in Equity — Opening Equity + Net Profit − Drawings = Closing Equity
  3. Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position) — Assets = Liabilities + Equity at a point in time
  4. Cash Flow Statement — Operating + Investing + Financing activities = Net change in cash

6. Depreciation Methods

  • Straight-Line Method (SLM) — Depreciation = (Cost − Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life
  • Diminishing Balance / Written-Down Value (WDV) — Depreciation = Book Value × Rate %
  • Units of Production — Depreciation = (Cost − Residual) ÷ Total Units × Units Produced

Nepal uses Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS) based on IFRS. ICAN exam questions often test the conceptual framework and the distinction between capital and revenue expenditure.

7. Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

The BRS reconciles the cash book balance with the bank statement balance. Common differences:

  • Outstanding Cheques — issued but not yet presented to bank
  • Deposits in Transit — recorded in cash book but not yet credited by bank
  • Bank Charges / Interest — charged by bank but not yet entered in cash book
  • Direct Credits / Debits — ECS, dividends received directly by bank
Adjusted Bank Balance = Adjusted Cash Book Balance = True Cash Balance

9. Suspense Account

When a trial balance does not agree, the difference is placed in a Suspense Account to allow financial statements to be prepared. Each error found later is corrected with a journal entry that clears the suspense account.

Quick Recall — DEAD CLIC:
Debit = Expenses, Assets, Drawings  |  Credit = Liabilities, Income, Capital

10. Inventory Valuation

Under NFRS / IAS 2, inventories are measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV).

  • FIFO — First In, First Out: oldest stock is sold first
  • Weighted Average Cost (WAC) — divides total cost by total units
  • LIFO is not permitted under NFRS.

NRV = Expected Selling Price − Estimated Costs of Completion and Sale

11. Provisions, Contingent Liabilities & Assets

  • Provision — recognised when: (a) present obligation, (b) probable outflow, (c) reliable estimate. Recognise in the Balance Sheet.
  • Contingent Liability — possible obligation; only disclosed in notes (unless remote, then ignored).
  • Contingent Asset — only disclosed when inflow is probable; recognised when virtually certain.
