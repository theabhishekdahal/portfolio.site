Question 1 · Basic Sums
ABC Co. has taken a 6-month loan from their foreign collaborators for US Dollars 2 million. Interest payable on maturity is at LIBOR plus 1.0%. Current 6-month LIBOR is 2%.

Enquiries regarding exchange rates with their bank elicit the following information:
Spot USD 148.5275
6 months forward48.4575
Required:
(i) What would be their total commitment in Rupees, if they enter into a forward contract? (ii) Will you advise them to do so? Explain giving reasons.
Ans: 98.36 m
Part (i):
$ payable in 6 month time = 2 + (2 × 3100 × 612) = $ 2.03m
Applicable rate under forward control 1$ = Rs. 48.4575
So, Rupee commitment = 2.03 × 48.4575 = Rs. 98.37m
Part (ii):
If the firm enters into forward contract, then it commits itself with outflow of Rs. 98.37m.
It will be protected against any rise in dollar value during period but won't be able to take advantage of any fall in dollar value.
So, the firm is recommended to go for forward contract if it expects appreciation in dollar value in 6 month time. But if fall is anticipated then the firm may choose to remain without hedging.
However, staying unhedged in anticipation of fall in dollar value is a risky strategy.
✦ What makes this question special

This introduces the very basics of forward hedging for foreign currency loans. Note how the interest is calculated in the foreign currency first, and then the total maturity amount is converted at the forward rate.

Question 6 · Basic Sums
Fleur du lac, a French co, has shipped goods to an American importer, which calls for payment at the end of 90 days. The invoice is for $1,24,000. Presently the exchange rate is 5.70 French francs to the $. If the French franc were to strengthen by 5% by the end of 90 days, what would be the transactions gain or loss to exporter in French francs?

If it were to weaken by 5%, what would happen?
Ans: Loss = 33,480 FRF; Gain = 37,200 FRF
Spot rate 1 $ = 5.70 French
Case I: Appreciation 5% on French franc
1 + 1 × 5% = 5.70
Or, 1.05 $ = 5.70 French
Therefore, 1 $ = 5.429 French (≈ 5.43 French)
Transaction gain/loss = 124,000 × (5.43 - 5.70)
= -33,480
Case II: Depreciation by 5%
1 - 1 × 5% = 5.70
Or, 0.95 $ = 5.70 FF
Therefore, 1 $ = 6 FF
Transaction gain/loss = 124,000 × (6 - 5.70)
= 37,200
✦ What makes this question special

A classic test of "Siegel's Paradox". A 5% strengthening/weakening of the base currency (Francs) must be computed by adjusting the foreign currency units ($) required to buy it, rather than applying a flat 5% to the 5.70 rate directly.

Part II: Steps:-
