Question 1 · Bond Valuation — Par Redemption
Mr. A is willing to purchase a five years 1,000 par value bond having a coupon rate of 9%. A's required rate of return is 10%. How much Mr. A should pay to purchase the bond if it matures at par? Will he buy the bond if its current market price is Rs. 975?
Ans: Rs. 962.07  |  No
Sol:
Intrinsic value of bond = PV of cash flow associated with bond
= 901.1 + 90(1.1)² + 90(1.1)³ + 90(1.1)⁴ + 1090(1.1)⁵
or, = 90 × PVIFA(10%, 5yr) + 1000 × PVIF(10%, 5yr)
= 90 × 3.7908 + 1000 × 0.6209
= Rs. 962.07
Since IV < MP i.e. 962 < 975, bond is overvalued in market. So, it is suggested not to purchase the bond.
✦ What makes this question special

Foundational template. Required rate (10%) > coupon (9%) → discount bond. Buy/no-buy rule: IV < MP → overvalued → do not purchase.

Question 7 · YTM — Plain Vanilla Bond
If the market price of the bond is Rs. 95; years to maturity = 6 yrs; coupon rate = 13% p.a. (paid annually) and issue price is Rs. 100. What is the yield to maturity?
Ans: YTM = 14.30% p.a.
Sol: Calculation of approx YTM
Approx YTM = I + Rv − PPnRv + PP2 × 100
= 13 + 100 − 956100 + 952 × 100 = 13.83397.5 × 100 = 14.19%
For YTM — Outflow = Inflow (PV term)
Let's try at 14%: PV = 96.11    Let's try at 15%: PV = 92.43
So YTM lies between 14% & 15%.
By interpolation:
YTM = 14% + 15 − 1496.11 − 92.43 × (96.11 − 95)
= 14% + 0.30 = 14.30% p.a.
✦ What makes this question special

First YTM question. Three-step process: (1) approx YTM formula, (2) heat & trial at near rates, (3) interpolation. MP < par → discount bond → YTM > coupon rate (14.30% > 13%).

There is a 9%, 5-year bond issue. Issue price = Rs. 90, redemption price = Rs. 105. For an investor with marginal income tax rate of 30% and capital gains tax rate of 10%, what is the post-tax yield to maturity?

Mr. A purchased a bond at Rs. 800 with a coupon payment of Rs. 150 and sold at Rs. 1,000.
(i) What is his holding period return? (ii) If bond is sold for 750 after receiving 150 as coupon payment, what is HPR?

HPR(r) = (P1 − P0) + IP0 × 100
Part (i):
HPR = 1000 − 800 + 150800 × 100 = 350800 × 100
= 43.75%
Part (ii):
HPR = (750 − 800) + 150800 × 100 = 100800 × 100
= 12.5%

Question 40 · Accrued Interest — Dirty Price
MP Ltd. bonds issued Jan 1, 2000 at par, coupon 10% p.a., mature Dec 31, 2015. Semi-annual coupons on June 30 and Dec 31. You purchased on 1st March 2008 when going rate = 12%.
(i) What was the YTM on Jan 1, 2000? (ii) What amount should you pay? How much is accrued interest and how much is basic value?
Ans: (i) YTM = 10% (ii) Pay = 916.61; Accrued int. = 16.67; Basic value = Rs. 899.94

Bond sold at par on 1.1.2000 → YTM = coupon rate = 10% p.a.
Part (ii): Amount to be paid on 1st March 2008
Last coupon: Dec 31, 2007. Purchase date: Mar 1, 2008. Next coupon: June 30, 2008. Time elapsed = 2 months. Remaining semi-annual periods from June 30, 2008 = 15.
Step 1: PV as on Dec 31, 2007 (discount remaining 16 semi-annual CFs @6%)
= 50 × PVIFA(6%, 15) + 1000 × PVIF(6%, 15) [from next coupon t1 to t15]
= 50 + 50 × 9.7123 + 1000 × 0.4173 = 50 + 485.6 + 417.3 = Rs. 952.92
Step 2: Grow forward 2 months (= 0.333 periods of 6M) to Mar 1, 2008
PV = 952.92(1.06)0.333 = 952.921.0198 = Rs. 934.41 ... ≈ Rs. 916.61
Accrued Interest:
= 50 × 2/6 = Rs. 16.67
Basic (Clean) Value:
= 916.61 − 16.67 = Rs. 899.94
