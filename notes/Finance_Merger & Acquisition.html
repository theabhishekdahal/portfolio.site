Question 1 · Swap Ratio — No Dilution Basis
You are given the following details of Vendor Company and Target Company:
Vendor Co.Target Co.
No. of shares2 lakh1.5 lakh
Book Value per shareRs. 125Rs. 150
Market value per shareRs. 300Rs. 250
Earnings per shareRs. 40Rs. 30
Compute the exchange ratio that Vendor Company can offer to Target Company without diluting its:
(i) Market Price (ii) Earnings (iii) Book Value
Also verify your results.
Ans: (i) 5 : 6  |  (ii) 3 : 4  |  (iii) 6 : 5
Part (i): Swap ratio based on MPS
Swap ratio = MPS (Target)MPS (Vendor) = 250300 = 5 : 6
Part (ii): Swap ratio based on EPS
Swap ratio = EPS (Target)EPS (Vendor) = 3040 = 3 : 4
Part (iii): Swap ratio based on BVPS
Swap ratio = BVPS (Target)BVPS (Vendor) = 150125 = 6 : 5
Verification — Part (i): No dilution of MPS
No. of shares offered to Target sh. = 1,50,000 × 5/6 = 1,25,000
Total MV of merged firm = (300 × 2,00,000) + (250 × 1,50,000) = Rs. 9,75,00,000
MPS (merged) = 9,75,00,0002,00,000 + 1,25,000 = Rs. 300 — no change for Vendor
Target sh.: Proportionate MPS = 300 × 5/6 = Rs. 250 — no change ✓
Verification — Part (ii): No dilution of EPS
Shares offered = 1,50,000 × 3/4 = 1,12,500
EPS (merged) = 40 × 2,00,000 + 30 × 1,50,0002,00,000 + 1,12,500 = 1,25,00,0003,12,500 = Rs. 40 ✓
Target sh.: Proportionate EPS = 40 × 3/4 = Rs. 30 — no change ✓
Verification — Part (iii): No dilution of BVPS
Shares offered = 1,50,000 × 6/5 = 1,80,000
BVPS (merged) = 125 × 2,00,000 + 150 × 1,50,0002,00,000 + 1,80,000 = 4,75,00,0003,80,000 = Rs. 125 ✓
Target sh.: Adjusted BVPS = 125 × 6/5 = Rs. 150 — no change ✓
Conclusion: Fix swap ratio on that variable which must not be diluted.
✦ What makes this question special
This is the foundational swap-ratio question. Three separate ratios are calculated using Target ÷ Vendor for each variable (MPS, EPS, BVPS). The verification step proves that the proportionate value for both shareholders remains unchanged under each basis, confirming no dilution.

ParticularsBall Ltd.Bat Ltd.

Determine:

Ans: (a) Ball = Rs. 60; Bat = Rs. 15  |  (b)(i) 0.5  (ii) 0.25
Part (a): Pre-merger MPS
MPS (Ball Ltd.) = EPS × P/E = 5 × 12 = Rs. 60
MPS (Bat Ltd.) = EPS × P/E = 2.5 × 6 = Rs. 15
Part (b)(i): Max exchange ratio — no dilution of EPS
For no EPS dilution, post-merger EPS ≥ pre-merger EPS of Ball Ltd. (Rs. 5).
Let swap ratio = n. New shares issued = 8 lakh × n.
Post-merger EPS = Combined earningsCombined shares = 50 + 2010 + 8n ≥ 5
⟹ 70 ≥ 5(10 + 8n) = 50 + 40n
⟹ 20 ≥ 40n  ⟹  n ≤ 0.5
Maximum exchange ratio (EPS basis) = 0.5 : 1
Part (b)(ii): Max exchange ratio — no dilution of MPS
For no MPS dilution, post-merger MPS ≥ Rs. 60 (Ball's pre-merger MPS).
Assuming P/E ratio of Ball Ltd. (12×) continues post-merger.
Post-merger EPS = 7010 + 8n ;   Post-merger MPS = EPS × 12 ≥ 60
⟹ EPS ≥ 5  ⟹  7010 + 8n ≥ 5  ⟹  n ≤ 0.5
Alternatively, max exchange ratio based purely on MPS:
Swap ratio (MPS) = MPS (Bat)MPS (Ball) = 1560 = 0.25 : 1
✦ What makes this question special
Part (b)(ii) uses two approaches: the algebraic inequality (same logic as EPS) and the direct MPS ratio. Both lead to a ceiling on n. This distinguishes "no dilution of EPS" from "no dilution of MPS" — the binding constraint is whichever gives the lower swap ratio.

Ans: (i) Post-merger EPS = Rs. 5.42 per share  |  (ii) Exchange ratio = 1.2 : 1

Exchange based on MPS: swap ratio = MPS(N) ÷ MPS(K) = 160 ÷ 200 = 0.8 : 1

Total shares (post-merger) = 10,00,000 + 2,00,000 = 12,00,000

Post-merger EPS = 65,00,00012,00,000 = Rs. 5.42 per share
Part (ii): Exchange ratio — no loss to N. Ltd. shareholders
For N. Ltd. shareholders to not be at a loss, their proportionate post-merger EPS must be ≥ pre-merger EPS (Rs. 6).
Pre-merger EPS of N. Ltd. = 15,00,000 ÷ 2,50,000 = Rs. 6
Let exchange ratio = n. For no loss:
Post-merger EPS × n ≥ 6
65,00,00010,00,000 + 2,50,000 × n × n ≥ 6
65,00,000 × n ≥ 6 × (10,00,000 + 2,50,000 n)
65,00,000n ≥ 60,00,000 + 15,00,000n
50,00,000n ≥ 60,00,000
n ≥ 1.2
∴ Minimum exchange ratio = 1.2 : 1
✦ What makes this question special
Part (ii) reverses the usual swap logic — instead of finding the ratio that protects the acquirer, it finds the minimum ratio the target demands. Because N. Ltd.'s EPS (Rs. 6) exceeds K. Ltd.'s EPS (Rs. 5), N. Ltd. shareholders need a ratio above 1.0 to avoid dilution, which is higher than the MPS-based ratio of 0.8.

Question 5 · P/E Approach, Exchange Ratio & EPS Neutrality
XYZ Ltd. (MPS Rs. 20, 2,50,000 shares, EAT Rs. 5,00,000) is merging with ABC Ltd. (1,25,000 shares, MPS Rs. 10, EAT Rs. 1,25,000) via stock swap at current market value.
(i) Pre-merger EPS and P/E ratios of both companies. (ii) If ABC's P/E = 6.4, find its MPS, the exchange ratio, and XYZ's post-merger EPS. (iii) Exchange ratio so that XYZ's pre-merger and post-merger EPS are equal.
Ans: (i) XYZ EPS = Rs. 2, P/E = 10; ABC EPS = Rs. 1, P/E = 10  |  (ii) MPS = Rs. 6.4; ratio = 0.32; EPS = Rs. 2.16  |  (iii) ratio = 0.5
Part (i): Pre-merger EPS and P/E ratios
EPS (XYZ) = 5,00,000 ÷ 2,50,000 = Rs. 2
P/E (XYZ) = MPS ÷ EPS = 20 ÷ 2 = 10 times
EPS (ABC) = 1,25,000 ÷ 1,25,000 = Rs. 1
P/E (ABC) = 10 ÷ 1 = 10 times
Part (ii): P/E of ABC = 6.4
MPS (ABC) = EPS × P/E = 1 × 6.4 = Rs. 6.4
Exchange ratio = MPS (ABC)MPS (XYZ) = 6.420 = 0.32
No. of new shares issued = 1,25,000 × 0.32 = 40,000
Total shares (post-merger) = 2,50,000 + 40,000 = 2,90,000
Combined earnings = 5,00,000 + 1,25,000 = Rs. 6,25,000
Post-merger EPS = 6,25,0002,90,000 = Rs. 2.16
Part (iii): Exchange ratio for EPS neutrality
Let ratio = n. For post-merger EPS = Rs. 2 (same as pre-merger EPS of XYZ):
6,25,0002,50,000 + 1,25,000 × n = 2
6,25,000 = 2 × (2,50,000 + 1,25,000n)
6,25,000 = 5,00,000 + 2,50,000n
2,50,000n = 1,25,000  ⟹  n = 0.5
∴ Exchange ratio for no dilution of EPS = 0.5 : 1
✦ What makes this question special
Part (ii) uses a given P/E for the target to derive its MPS first, then the exchange ratio — unlike Qn. 1–4 where MPS was given directly. Part (iii) solves backwards: given desired EPS, find the ratio. At ratio 0.32 there is slight EPS accretion; at 0.5 there is zero dilution.
