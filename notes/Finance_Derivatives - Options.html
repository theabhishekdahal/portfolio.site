Question 1 · Option Basics

A Call Option at a strike price of 280 is selling at a premium of 23. At what share price on maturity will it break even for the buyer of the option?

Will the writer of the option also break-even at the same price?

Ans: Rs. 303, Yes
Breakeven price for call option buyer:
= Exercise price + Premium paid
= 280 + 23
= Rs. 303
Writer's breakeven:
Yes, writer of the option also has the same breakeven price of Rs. 303.
✦ What makes this question special

The simplest BEP question: for a call, BEP = Exercise Price + Premium. The writer's BEP is identical to the buyer's — both break even at the same spot price at maturity.

Question 7 · Fair Value — General Approach

By using the following data calculate theoretical value of 6 months call option.

Strike price= Rs. 100
Current price of one share= Rs. 110
Rate of interest= 10% p.a.
Dividend receivable after 4 months= Rs. 5
Ans: Rs. 9.92
Step 1: Adjusted spot price (remove PV of dividend)
PV of dividend = 51 + 0·1 × 4/12 = 51·0333 = Rs. 4.839
Adjusted spot = 110 − 4.839 = Rs. 105.161
Step 2: FV of adjusted spot price after 6 months
= 105.161 × (1 + 0·1 × 6/12) = 105.161 × 1·05 = Rs. 110.42
Step 3: Expected payoff (call option, EP = 100):
= 110.42 − 100 = Rs. 10.42
Step 4: Fair option premium (PV of payoff):
= 10.42(1 + 0·1 × 6/12) = 10.421·050
= Rs. 9.92
✦ What makes this question special

General Approach question with dividend. Key step: first remove the PV of dividend from the current spot price to get the dividend-adjusted spot. Then compute FV of the adjusted spot over the full option period. Payoff = adjusted FV − strike. Fair premium = PV of payoff discounted at risk-free rate.

As per Black Scholes model
Value of call option = S × N(d1) − K·e−rn × N(d2)
= 185 × N(d1) − 170 × e−0.07×3 × N(d2)   [W.N. 1]

= 159.8955 − 108.20
= Rs. 51.70

d1 = ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2) × nσ · √n
= ln(185/170) + (0.07 + (0.18)2/2) × 30.18 × √3
= [0.0848 + (0.07 + 0.0162) × 3] ÷ (0.18 × 1.7321)
= [0.0848 + 0.2586] ÷ 0.3118

Question 30 · Residuals — Intrinsic Value & Time Value

The stock of ABC Ltd. is currently selling for Rs. 830. Consider the option quote information of ABC Ltd.'s stock shown below:

Expiration Strike Price (Rs.) Calls Puts
VolumeLast VolumeLast
Ashadh800230281608
Shrawan8001706012714
Kartik80013980.504339
Magh800601023336.5
(i)Are the call options in the money? What is the intrinsic value of a call option? (ii)Are the put options in the money? What is the intrinsic value of a put option? (iii)Which Options are mispriced and why? Explain how you could profit from mispricing in each case?
Ans: (i) Rs. 30 (ii) Nil (iii) Ashadh Call and Magh Put
Part (i): Call option
Intrinsic value = pay off = SP − EP = 830 − 800 = Rs. 30
All call options are "in the money" as SP > EP.
Part (ii): Put option
IV = pay off = 0 (800 − 830 = −30 → negative, so lapsed)
All put options are "out of money" as SP > EP. IV = Nil.
Part (iii): Mispriced options
Ashadh call option trading below its intrinsic value:
In this case IV = Rs. 30, but option premium = Rs. 28
So, time value = 30 − 28 = −2
This is an indication that Ashadh call option is undervalued. So, one should buy Ashadh call to make some profit.
Magh's put option trading below Kartik's put option:
All put options have IV = 0 (SP > EP, so puts are out of the money).
So the price quoted (premium quoted) is a result of time value only. Time value should have been higher for Magh put as there exist additional risk of three months compared to Kartik's put. Therefore, Kartik put may have been overvalued & Magh put may have been undervalued. So, one should buy Magh's put & sell Kartik put option to earn profit.
✦ What makes this question special

Tests intrinsic value (IV) and time value concepts. For calls: IV = max(SP − EP, 0). For puts: IV = max(EP − SP, 0). Mispricing: if option trades below IV, it's undervalued (buy it). Magh put anomaly: further expiry should have higher time value than shorter expiry.

Question 20 · Call Put Parity Model

Aptex Ltd. has both call and put options traded on NSE. Both options have an expiration date 6 months and exercise price of 30. The call and put are currently selling for Rs. 4 respectively. If the risk free rate of interest is 6% p.a., what would be the stock price of Aptex. Ltd.? [Given PVIF (6%, 0.5 yrs) = 0.9709].

Ans: Price = Rs. 29.13
As per call put parity approach
S + P0 = C0 + PV of K
Or, S + 4 = 4 + 30 × PVIF (6%, 0.5yr)
Or, S = 30 × 0.9709
∴ S = Rs. 29.13
Hence, stock price = Rs. 29.13
✦ What makes this question special

Call and put prices are equal (both Rs. 4), so they cancel from both sides of the parity equation, leaving S = PV of K directly. Uses PVIF table value rather than computing discount factor.
